# Authoritative simulation

The server owns one persisted timeline. `clock.initialEpochMs + gameTimeHours` is the civil time shown to clients. At normal speed one real second advances one simulation second. Debug controls may set the civil epoch, simulation multiplier, or the independent movement multiplier; epoch changes increase a generation counter so clients snap cleanly.

The monotonic scheduler accumulates elapsed time and integrates it in fixed 100 ms steps. A busy callback retains its debt instead of discarding simulation. Process downtime is deliberately not replayed. Each step advances income, movement, extraction, construction, warhead accrual, production, combat, capture, and periodic AI in that order. `pendingHours` and `simulationTick` persist, so restore preserves fractional work and periodic phase.

Movement follows the land graph and keeps the physical occupied edge after stop, split, combat, or redirect. A mid-edge order compares routes through both endpoints. Cross-landmass routes may use explicit sea edges with timed embarking, transit, and disembarking; naval stacks cannot fight or accept replacement orders mid-crossing. Every traversed land edge is revalidated against current relations. Required transit wars are returned together and applied only after the client confirms their complete union. Pursuit uses the target's current occupied edge while detected and its last known position afterward. Swept contact prevents a large step from passing through a hostile force.

The projection includes the sampled position, remaining road polyline, next target, duration, timestamp, and clock generation. Clients buffer authoritative samples, traverse every verified corner, extrapolate for at most 500 ms, and freeze stale state. Unknown route corrections snap to the new sample rather than inventing travel across terrain. Picking uses the same presented trajectory.

Close combat applies damage continuously every fixed step. Each side has ten frontage slots; selected firepower grows sublinearly with force size, while all units remain in the target HP pool. Damage is calculated from one pre-damage snapshot and applied simultaneously. With full-health infantry and no retreat route, the balance target is about two real hours for 1 versus 1, substantially shorter for 10 versus 1, and longer for 10 versus 10. Tests pin those relationships and step-size invariance.

Retreat routes are friendly-only and directional. A withdrawing army remains protected until it has passed its first escape node and is outside hostile contact range. Artillery applies continuous ranged damage only while stationary, never selects destroyed targets, and emits presentation pulses at one-second cadence.

Capture, extraction, production merging, and rally spawning require physical arrival at a graph node. Capture clears incompatible queues and rally points and repairs extraction ownership. Queue overflow carries into later orders within the same step. Rally points require a legal friendly route and cannot declare war.

Fog projections expose only server-derived facts. Own province projections include exact production, construction and rally capabilities; own armies include extraction and retreat capabilities. The browser records commands as pending intent and never deducts resources, changes orders, or inserts queues before an authoritative revision arrives.

`GameState` stores countries, territory, queues, rallies, armies and occupied/naval edges, battles/fronts, deposits, diplomacy, strategic devastation, timeline fields, and monotonic counters. Restore discards unknown legacy fields, defaults additive fields, then validates schema, world references, graph paths and edge geometry, queue ownership/progress, battle membership, extraction links, diplomacy records, and counters.
